Our Story

Started With One Bag, One Table

In 2011, Elena Voss left a role designing handbags for a large fashion house to open a single work table outside Florence. She wanted to make one bag exceptionally well, rather than fifty adequately. That first satchel — cut, stitched, and finished by her own hands — became The Vesper, still our best-selling silhouette today.

Thirteen years later, Atelier Noir remains a small team: seven artisans, one workshop, and a refusal to outsource a single stitch. We release new colorways each season, but we've only ever added six silhouettes to our permanent line — each one earned its place.

Craftsmanship

From Hide To Handbag

Every bag passes through the same four stages, each performed by hand by the same artisan from start to finish.

  1. Hide Selection

    Full-grain hides are inspected by hand for grain consistency and natural markings — roughly 1 in 3 is rejected.

  2. Pattern & Cut

    Brass patterns, some over a decade old, guide each cut to minimize waste and keep proportions exact.

  3. Saddle Stitching

    Two needles, one thread, pulled through by hand — a stitch that won't unravel even if a single thread is cut.

  4. Hardware & Finish

    Solid brass hardware is set, edges are hand-burnished, and the finished bag is numbered and inspected.

Materials

What We Use, And What We Don't

Full-Grain, Always

We never use split or bonded leather. Full-grain hides develop a patina over years of use instead of cracking.

Vegetable Tanning

Where the silhouette allows, we choose vegetable over chrome tanning, cutting water use significantly.

Solid Brass Hardware

No plated zinc. Our clasps and feet are solid brass, hand-polished, and built to be re-polished for decades.
